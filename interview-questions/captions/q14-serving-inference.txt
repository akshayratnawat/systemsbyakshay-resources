AI Engineering Interview Question 14

Your inference cost per request tripled after a "minor" model upgrade with no accuracy gain reported by the vendor. How do you investigate and what levers do you pull?

What this actually tests: Cost forensics discipline, not taking a vendor's framing at face value.

How a strong answer breaks down:
→ Don't trust "minor" at face value - pull the actual billing breakdown: is the 3x from per-token price, token volume, or request volume.
→ Check if the new model version is simply more verbose by default, since longer completions multiply cost per request even at the same price per token.
→ Check if pricing tiers changed - a 'minor' model bump sometimes moves the account into a different price bracket entirely.
→ Check for retry/fallback logic silently multiplying requests, if the new model has a different error rate.

🚩 Red flag: Accepting "minor upgrade, no accuracy change" from the vendor without independently verifying via billing data

🔁 Likely follow-up: You find the new model is 40% more verbose per response and that's most of the 3x. Capping max_tokens hard-truncates some answers mid-sentence. What's your actual fix?
